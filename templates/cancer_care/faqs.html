1. Diagnosis, Pathology & Staging

The risk of needle-track seeding is exceedingly minute (<0.001%), while the benefit of obtaining definitive molecular markers (HER2, ER/PR, EGFR, PD-L1) is essential for curing the disease.
Clinical staging (cTNM) is estimated prior to surgery using physical examinations, biopsy results, and imaging scans (CT, PET-CT, MRI). Pathological staging (pTNM) is determined after surgery by direct microscopic histopathological examination of the excised tumor and resected lymph nodes, providing the most accurate assessment of disease extent.

2. Chemotherapy, Immunotherapy & Side Effects

Chemotherapy works by directly damaging and killing rapidly dividing cells throughout the body (which causes side effects like temporary hair loss and nausea). Immunotherapy (checkpoint inhibitors like Pembrolizumab) does not poison cells directly; instead, it blocks the molecular "disguise" (PD-L1) cancer cells use to hide, unleashing the patient's own cytotoxic T-cells to eradicate the tumor.
A body temperature of ≥100.4°F (38.0°C) during active chemotherapy is a critical emergency known as Febrile Neutropenia. Because chemotherapy lowers white blood cell counts, a minor infection can rapidly become life-threatening. If you develop a fever or severe chills, do NOT take paracetamol to mask the fever — contact our 24/7 oncology emergency helpline (1800-SPHERIX-CA) or visit the emergency room immediately.
A chemoport is a small titanium or plastic reservoir placed beneath the skin of the upper chest under local anesthesia. It connects to a central vein, allowing nurses to administer chemotherapy infusions, fluids, and draw blood without repeatedly sticking arm veins. This protects peripheral arm veins from chemical irritation (phlebitis) and extravasation injury.

3. Nutrition, Recovery & Lifestyle

Cutting out all dietary carbohydrates will not starve cancer cells; instead, it leads to rapid muscle wasting (cachexia) and malnutrition. While limiting refined sugars is good for weight control, adequate healthy nutrition is mandatory during active treatment.

Regular light exercise reduces cancer-related fatigue by up to 40%, improves sleep, and boosts chemotherapy tolerance.
