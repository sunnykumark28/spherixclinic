Multidisciplinary Therapeutics

Cancer Treatment Modalities & Pathways

Modern cancer care combines targeted local therapies (surgery and radiation) with systemic precision treatments (immunotherapy, targeted antibodies, and chemotherapy) calibrated to each tumor's unique genomic footprint.

Multidisciplinary Clinical Governance Requirement

At Spherix Comprehensive Cancer Centre, patient-specific treatment plans must be formulated and approved by authorized healthcare professionals within an accredited Multidisciplinary Tumor Board (MDT). Treatment regimens are tailored according to NCCN and ESMO clinical guidelines, factoring in histology, molecular biomarkers, performance status, and patient values.

1. Surgical Oncology & Reconstruction

Curative resection, cytoreduction, and minimally invasive robotic interventions

Curative Radical Resection Surgical excision of the primary malignant tumor along with a wide margin of healthy tissue and systematic regional lymph node dissection (e.g. mastectomy, colectomy, lobectomy).
Robotic & VATS Techniques da Vinci Xi robotic systems and video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery providing 3D high-magnification visualization, minimal blood loss, and accelerated return of organ function.
Microvascular Reconstruction Immediate autologous free-flap tissue transfers (e.g. DIEP flap breast reconstruction, free fibula mandibular restoration) ensuring optimal functional and aesthetic survivorship.

2. Medical Oncology & Systemic Chemotherapy

Neoadjuvant downstaging, adjuvant consolidation, and anti-emetic protocols

Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy Administered prior to surgery to shrink locally advanced tumors, convert inoperable disease to resectable status, and eradicate early microscopic metastases.
Adjuvant Chemotherapy Delivered post-operatively to eliminate occult residual micrometastatic disease and dramatically diminish long-term systemic cancer recurrence.
Toxicity Mitigation Modern 3-drug neurokinin/serotonin antiemetic regimens prevent >90% of nausea; cold caps reduce alopecia; G-CSF growth factors protect against neutropenia.

3. Radiation Oncology & Radiosurgery

TrueBeam IMRT, Stereotactic CyberKnife, and 3D Brachytherapy

Intensity-Modulated Radiotherapy (IMRT) Computer-controlled linear accelerators sculpt ionizing radiation beams precisely around tumor contours while sparing critical adjacent healthy organs (heart, spinal cord).
SBRT / CyberKnife Radiosurgery Ablative high-dose radiation delivered in 1-5 sessions with sub-millimeter tracking accuracy for early-stage lung, liver, prostate, and oligometastatic lesions.
High-Dose-Rate (HDR) Brachytherapy Radioactive sources placed directly into or adjacent to the tumor (e.g. cervix, prostate), delivering concentrated tumor doses with rapid dose fall-off in surrounding tissue.

4. Immunotherapy & CAR-T Cellular Therapies

Unleashing the patient's own cytotoxic immune system to eradicate tumors

Immune Checkpoint Blockade Monoclonal antibodies (Pembrolizumab, Nivolumab, Atezolizumab) that block PD-1/PD-L1 or CTLA-4 receptors, restoring T-cell capacity to recognize and kill cancer cells.
Chimeric Antigen Receptor (CAR) T-Cells Autologous T-cells engineered in specialized cell labs with synthetic CAR receptors targeting CD19 or BCMA, producing complete remissions in refractory leukemias and lymphomas.
Bispecific Antibodies (BiTEs) Dual-affinity antibodies that bridge cytotoxic T-cells directly to malignant surface antigens, inducing targeted immunological tumor lysis.

5. Targeted Molecular Therapy & ADCs

Disrupting oncogenic signaling cascades and precision missile payloads

Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs) Small-molecule oral drugs (Osimertinib, Alectinib, Imatinib, Lenvatinib) that inhibit mutant oncogenic kinases, stopping cellular proliferation.
Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs) Monoclonal antibodies chemically linked to potent cytotoxic payloads (e.g. T-DXd / Enhertu, Sacituzumab govitecan) that deliver chemotherapy directly inside cancer cells.
PARP Inhibitors Exploiting synthetic lethality in tumors harboring homologous recombination repair mutations (BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2) in breast, ovarian, and prostate cancers.

6. Hormone & Endocrine Therapy

Starving hormone-dependent malignancies of growth stimulation

Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulators Tamoxifen blocks estrogen receptors in ER-positive breast cancer, drastically diminishing 10-year recurrence rates for pre- and post-menopausal women.
Aromatase Inhibitors (AIs) Letrozole, Anastrozole, and Exemestane stop peripheral estrogen synthesis in postmenopausal women, preventing hormone-driven tumor growth.
Androgen Deprivation Therapy (ADT) GnRH agonists (Leuprolide) and novel AR pathway inhibitors (Enzalutamide, Abiraterone) suppress testosterone to castrate levels in prostate cancer.
